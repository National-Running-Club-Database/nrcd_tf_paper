Nationals Team Points Maximization — Clearing-Capable Rosters
=============================================================

Men's vs. Women's Teams — Maximize Total Championship Points (Not Scorer Count)

Based on 2024–2026 NIRCA outdoor data (RQ1B thresholds, RQ1C nationals-margin
comparisons, national clearing-pool sizes). Relay-inclusive; March 1+ per season.

DATA NOTE: Distance / steeplechase use corrected WA from new_steeplechase_data
(steeple 8th-place ≈740 men / ≈752 women). Sprints, hurdles, jumps, throws
unchanged from relays_findings. All five event groups included.

Assumption: Many athletes per event group (both genders) already clear the
8th-place WA threshold for every event in that group.


================================================================================
HOW THIS REPORT DIFFERS FROM THE SCORER-COUNT FRAME
================================================================================

Maximizing athletes in the top 8 favors SPREADING clearing-capable talent across
events (more scoring lines). Maximizing TEAM POINTS favors CONCENTRATING depth
where your athletes can finish HIGH (1st–4th), not just clear the bar.

Standard track scoring (assumed 10-8-6-5-4-3-2-1 for places 1–8):
  • 1st place = 10 points; 8th place = 1 point
  • Four athletes placing 1st–4th in one event = 29 points
  • Four athletes placing 6th–8th in a crowded event = 9 points
  • One relay team placing 1st = 10 points (uses 4 athletes, one scoring line)

Decision logic for clearing-capable rosters:
  1. RQ1C margin — largest buffer above the 8th-place line predicts higher
     finish position, not just qualification.
  2. National clearing-pool size — fewer clearing athletes nationally = less
     competition for top places (where points are weighted).
  3. Depth economics — individual events allow up to 8 scoring lines per team;
     relays allow 1. With deep clearing rosters, stacking thin fields beats
     staffing one relay.
  4. Relay rule — staff ONE A relay per gender where margin is strongest
     (usually 4x100m); do not sink relay depth that could sweep individual
     events.


================================================================================
MEN'S TEAM — EVENT FOCUS BY GROUP
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sprints
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PRIMARY STACK: 400m
SECONDARY:    100m (margin outliers)
RELAY:        One 4x100m A team only
DEPRIORITIZE: 200m, 4x400m depth, crowded open-sprint concentration

Why 400m is the points play:
  • Smallest national open-sprint clearing pool (42 clearers vs. 65–72 for
    100m/200m and 133 for 4x100m).
  • Best open-sprint nationals margin: beats 200m (170–123) and narrowly beats
    100m (93–81) among dual athletes.
  • With many clearing-capable sprinters on your team, stacking 400m targets
    10+8+6+5 point bundles instead of 3+2+1 scraps in a deep field.

Why one 4x100m, not depth:
  • 4x100 wins margin vs. all open sprints (e.g. vs. 200m 188–60), so the A
    relay is worth ~10 points if competitive for the win.
  • But 133 athletes clear nationally — winning or placing 2nd is hard.
  • Four legs in 4x100 = one scoring opportunity. Those same four in 400m could
    yield four scoring lines if the national 400m field stays thin.

Why not 200m / 4x400:
  • 200m loses every margin comparison to 100m and 400m.
  • 4x400 has 81 national clearers and only modest margin gains over 400m; four
    athletes produce one relay score.

Points strategy: Enter 3–5 clearing-capable men in 400m. Staff one 4x100. Use
100m only for athletes whose margin clearly exceeds 400m. Treat 200m as
development, not a points target.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Distance
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PRIMARY STACK: 3000m Steeplechase
SECONDARY:    5000m
TERTIARY:     1500m
RELAY:        One 4x400m at most (milers who don't stack steeple/5K)
AVOID:        800m as a points target

Why steeplechase is the top men's distance points event:
  • Smallest distance clearing pool nationally (24 clearers).
  • Dominates every nationals-margin comparison (vs. 1500m 123–47, vs. 5000m
    77–32, vs. 800m 86–39).
  • Lowest individual distance bar under corrected WA (~740 vs. 786–829) —
    clearing-capable steeplers typically still have the largest margin in the
    group → best chance at 1st–3rd (10+8+6 points).

Why stack 5000m second:
  • Beats 1500m (225–209) and 800m (140–79) on margin; beats 4x400 (81–76).
  • Only 36 national clearers — room for multiple high placings from one team.
  • Bar ~786 vs. 827–829 for 1500m/800m — more separation above the line.

Why 1500m is tertiary, 800m avoided:
  • 1500m has the most distance clearers (51) — harder to pile up top places;
    use for milers without steeple/5K margin advantage.
  • 800m: highest bar (829 WA), loses all margin comparisons — clearing-capable
    800m runners are fighting for 1–2 points at best.

Points strategy: Concentrate clearing-capable distance men in steeple and 5K.
Use 1500m for overflow milers. One 4x400 for relay points if roster allows.
Do not spread depth into 800m.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hurdles
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PRIMARY STACK: 110m Hurdles
SECONDARY:    400m Hurdles
RELAY:        One 4x100m (speed hurdlers not stacking 110mH)

Why 110mH is the men's hurdle points focus:
  • Highest clear rate in men's track (32.5%) with only 38 national clearers —
    clearing-capable 110m hurdlers often have large margins above the ~629 WA
    bar (lowest among men's sprint/hurdle events).
  • Margin vs. relays is essentially even; vs. 400mH, 400mH wins head-to-head
    (42–27) — so long hurdlers go to 400mH, sprint hurdlers stack 110mH.

Why 400mH as secondary stack:
  • 27 national clearers (thin field). Best individual margin for dual hurdlers
    running both events.
  • Stack long-hurdle specialists here for 10+8+6 potential.

Why not 4x400 depth:
  • Large clearing pool (81), modest margin edge — points return poor vs.
    stacking both hurdle events.

Points strategy: Split clearing hurdlers — 110mH stack for sprint types, 400mH
stack for long types. One 4x100 from elite speed hurdlers if not already scoring
in 110mH.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Jumps
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PRIMARY STACK: Triple Jump
SECONDARY:    High Jump
AVOID:        Long Jump as a points concentration

Why triple jump:
  • Best nationals margin in the jump group (vs. long 61–19, vs. high 21–11).
  • Only 23 national clearers — thinnest horizontal jump field.
  • Lower bar than long jump (726 vs. 792 WA) → clearing-capable triple jumpers
    are positioned for top places, not 7th–8th.

Why high jump second:
  • 29 clearers nationally; 27.9% clear rate; beats long jump on margin (34–20).
  • Stack vertical jumpers separately from triple for parallel point bundles.

Why not long jump:
  • Most jump clearers nationally (34) but loses all margin comparisons and has
    the highest bar — clearing-capable long jumpers fight for low places (1–3
    points) in a crowded event.

Points strategy: Stack clearing-capable jumpers in triple and high jump. Use
long jump only when an athlete's margin there clearly exceeds triple/high.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Throws
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PRIMARY STACK: Shot Put
SECONDARY:    Discus
AVOID:        Javelin, Hammer

Why shot put:
  • Beats discus on nationals margin among dual throwers (67–45).
  • Beats relays when throwers cross over (shot vs. 4x100: 20–5).
  • 22 national clearers — thin enough to target 1st–4th with a deep roster.

Why discus second:
  • 25 clearers; strong margin vs. relays (13–2 vs. 4x100).
  • Stack dual throwers in both implements if roster depth allows separate
    point bundles (shot stack + discus stack).

Points strategy: Keep throwers in the circle. Concentrate clearing-capable men
in shot first; add discus depth for dual-competency athletes.


================================================================================
WOMEN'S TEAM — EVENT FOCUS BY GROUP
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sprints
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PRIMARY STACK: 400m
SECONDARY:    100m (margin outliers)
RELAY:        One 4x100m A team only
DEPRIORITIZE: 200m, 4x400m depth

Why 400m is the women's sprint points play:
  • Smallest open-sprint clearing pool (23 clearers vs. 27–25 for 100m/200m and
    105 for 4x100m).
  • Crushes 200m on margin (102–25); beats 100m (36–22).
  • Deep clearing rosters can target 10+8+6+5 in 400m vs. fighting 100+ national
    4x100 clearers for a single 10-point relay line.

Why one 4x100m:
  • Best relay margin (vs. 100m 124–12, vs. 400m 48–15) — A team still worth
    staffing for a shot at 10 points.
  • Do not stack the roster into 4x100; national field is too deep for reliable
    high places beyond the A team.

Why not 200m:
  • Loses margin to both 100m and 400m — worst open sprint for points.

Points strategy: Stack clearing-capable women in 400m (3–5 entries). One 4x100.
100m for athletes with clear 100m margin advantage. Minimal 200m investment.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Distance
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PRIMARY STACK: 3000m Steeplechase
SECONDARY:    5000m
TERTIARY:     1500m
RELAY:        One 4x400m (milers with 4x400 margin over 1500m)
AVOID:        800m as a points target

Why steeplechase:
  • Dominant margins vs. all distance events (vs. 1500m 75–6, vs. 5000m 40–4,
    vs. 800m 45–7). Beats 4x400 (30–23).
  • 23 national clearers — thin field for sweeping top places.
  • Corrected bar ~752 WA (was ~511 under old data) — still the lowest
    individual women's distance bar and the best margin buffers in the group.

Why stack 5000m second:
  • Beats 1500m (79–65) and 800m (58–27); beats 4x400 (42–29).
  • 29 national clearers — room for multiple high finishes.

Why 1500m tertiary, one 4x400:
  • 1500m has 46 national clearers — most crowded individual distance event;
    use for milers without steeple/5K advantage.
  • 4x400 beats 1500m on margin (74–58) — one relay for milers who don't stack
    steeple/5K is a reasonable ~10-point play.
  • 800m loses every margin comparison — avoid as a points concentration.

Points strategy: Steeple and 5K stacks first. 1500m for overflow. One 4x400.
No 800m depth strategy.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hurdles
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PRIMARY STACK: 400m Hurdles
SECONDARY:    100m Hurdles
RELAY:        One 4x100m (only if not stacking 400mH)

Why 400mH is the women's hurdle points centerpiece:
  • 40.0% national clear rate with only 32 clearers — best combination of margin
    and thin field among women's hurdle/sprint events.
  • Beats 100mH (26–18), beats 4x400 strongly (25–9), beats 4x100 (17–13).
  • Clearing-capable 400m hurdlers should almost all stack here for 10+8+6
    potential.

Why 100mH secondary:
  • For dedicated sprint hurdlers; 28 national clearers, accessible bar (~608).
  • Stack separately from 400mH — don't merge into one hurdle line.

Why not 4x400:
  • Loses margin to 400mH (9–25) — poor points return vs. individual stack.

Points strategy: Concentrate clearing-capable women hurdlers in 400mH. 100mH
stack for sprint-hurdle specialists. Relay only as overflow.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Jumps
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PRIMARY STACK: High Jump
SECONDARY:    Triple Jump
AVOID:        Long Jump as a points concentration

Why high jump:
  • Best women's jump margin: beats long (24–15), beats triple (12–6).
  • 28 national clearers; lowest jump bar (~681 WA).
  • Clearing-capable high jumpers are positioned for top places in a thin field.

Why triple jump second:
  • Beats long jump (33–13); 18 clearers nationally — second stack opportunity.
  • 31.6% clear rate — strong conversion for horizontal jumpers.

Why not long jump:
  • 26 clearers but loses margin to both high and triple; highest jump bar
    (~760 WA) — clearing-capable long jumpers land in lower places (1–4 pts).

Points strategy: Stack high jump first (women differ from men — high beats
triple on margin). Triple stack for horizontal specialists. Avoid long-jump
concentration.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Throws
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PRIMARY STACK: Discus
SECONDARY:    Shot Put
AVOID:        Javelin, Hammer

Why discus:
  • Lowest bar in women's track (~504 WA) — largest margin buffers.
  • Beats shot on margin (41–32); beats 4x400 (6–1).
  • 25 national clearers — ideal for 1st–4th point bundles.

Why shot put second:
  • 23 clearers; 20.0% clear rate — secondary stack for dual throwers.

Points strategy: Stack clearing-capable throwers in discus first; shot put for
additional depth. No javelin/hammer points strategy (zero national clearers).


================================================================================
CROSS-GENDER POINTS STRATEGY — CLEARING ROSTERS
================================================================================

Principle: STACK vs. SPREAD
---------------------------
  Scorer-count logic:     Spread athletes across events (maximize scoring lines)
  Points-maximization:    Stack thin, high-margin events (maximize place value)

Relay allocation
----------------
  • One 4x100m per gender (margin-strongest sprint relay).
  • Men: one 4x400m only if distance/hurdle roster allows without stealing
    steeple/5K/400mH stacks.
  • Women: one 4x400m for milers with relay margin over 1500m.
  • Never staff B/C relay depth at the expense of individual stacks.

Highest point-potential stacks (thin national clearing pool + strong margin)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Men:
    1. 3000m Steeplechase (24 clearers, dominant margins)
    2. Triple Jump (23 clearers, best jump margins)
    3. 400m (42 clearers, best open sprint margins)
    4. Shot Put (22 clearers, best throw margins)
    5. 5000m (36 clearers)
    6. 110m Hurdles (38 clearers, low bar)

  Women:
    1. 3000m Steeplechase (23 clearers, dominant margins)
    2. 400m (23 clearers, best open sprint margins)
    3. 400m Hurdles (32 clearers, 40% clear rate)
    4. High Jump (28 clearers, best jump margins)
    5. Discus (25 clearers, lowest bar)
    6. 5000m (29 clearers)

Events to avoid stacking (crowded national clearing pools — low place value)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Men:  4x100m (133), 4x400m (81), 200m (72), 1500m (51), 800m (46)
  Women: 4x100m (105), 4x400m (78), 1500m (46)

  Note: 4x100 still gets ONE A team per gender — just not depth concentration.
