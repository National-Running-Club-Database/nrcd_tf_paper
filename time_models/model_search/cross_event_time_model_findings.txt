Cross-Event Time Model Findings
===============================

Outdoor track data: 2024–2026, results on or after March 1.
Event groups: Sprints (100m / 200m / 400m) and Distance (800m / 1500m / 5000m).
Steeplechase excluded. All times in seconds (e.g. 267.7 = 4:27.7).

Always predict TIME in the target event, then convert to World Athletics points
in that event's table. Do not compare WA across events directly.

PART 1 — BEST PREDICTION FORMULAS (FULL SAMPLE)
================================================================================

Source: 5-fold CV model search (time_models/model_search/best_time_models.csv).
20 of 24 event pairs beat simple linear regression by >0.01s CV median |error|.
Largest gains: men 100m+200m→400m (−0.65s), men 800m+1500m→5000m (−16.1s).

Decision tree:
  1. Two known events bracketing the target → multivariate OLS (if available)
  2. One known event, long hop → binned_ratio or median_ratio
  3. One known event, inverse/endurance curve → robust_trimmed_linear or ratio_linear
  4. Else → linear_ols (strong for adjacent events)

Use CV median |error| as a typical ± uncertainty band around each prediction.

PART 2 — RACE VOLUME ANALYSIS (4, 5, 6 RACES PER SEASON)
================================================================================

Research question: Do time-prediction models become more accurate when
restricted to athletes who competed in more races during a season?

Source: time_models/model_search/race_count/
Script:  time_models/model_search/compare_time_models_by_race_count.py


Method
------
  • Unit: athlete-season (not pooled across 2024–2026).
  • Race count: unique valid results in the discipline CSV for that event group
    (same sources as build_cross_event_time_models.py).
  • Filters: all athlete-seasons, and exactly 4, 5, or 6 races.
  • Same 8+ candidate models and 5-fold CV as the full-sample search.
  • Primary metric: CV median absolute error (seconds). Lower is better.
  • Minimum n=20 athlete-season pairs per event pair to evaluate.


Headline comparison (mean CV median |error| across evaluated pairs)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Filter        Pairs   Mean linear   Mean best   Δ (linear−best)
all              24       8.139s       6.768s           1.372s
4 races          20       9.264s       7.092s           2.172s
5 races          16       6.711s       4.902s           1.809s
6 races          10       6.086s       4.931s           1.154s

Compared to all athlete-seasons (mean best CV = 6.768s):
  • 4 races: 7.092s — 0.324s LESS accurate (unreliable; see below)
  • 5 races: 4.902s — 1.865s MORE accurate
  • 6 races: 4.931s — 1.836s MORE accurate


Sample sizes (athlete-seasons in discipline CSV)
-----------------------------------------------
Filter     Men Sprints   Women Sprints   Men Distance   Women Distance
all           1,536            702            2,503            1,181
4 races         129             60              180               63
5 races          57             29               87               31
6 races          39             12               60               11

At 6 races, many women's pairs drop below n=20 — only 10 of 24 pairs could be
evaluated. Conclusions for women at 6 races are limited.


Key finding: Does more racing improve model accuracy?
------------------------------------------------------
YES — but unevenly. The clearest gains appear at 5–6 races, especially for
men's distance long hops. Sprint pairs improve modestly. The 4-race filter
is mixed and less reliable due to smaller samples and volatile long-hop pairs.


Exactly 4 races vs all athlete-seasons
--------------------------------------
  Pairs more accurate: 13/20
  Pairs less accurate:  7/20
  Mean Δ (4 minus all): +0.962s
  Median Δ:             −0.049s  (essentially flat)

  Interpretation: Median pair is unchanged; mean worsens because a few long-hop
  pairs with tiny n (e.g. men 800m→5000m at n=38: +11.4s error) are unstable.
  Sprint pairs mostly improve slightly; distance long hops are unreliable at 4.


Exactly 5 races vs all athlete-seasons
--------------------------------------
  Pairs more accurate: 7/16
  Pairs less accurate:  7/16
  Mean Δ (5 minus all): −0.205s
  Median Δ:             −0.004s  (~same)

  Notable improvements:
    Men 1500m→5000m:  23.1s → 18.8s  (−4.3s)
    Men 800m→5000m:   23.7s → 21.0s  (−2.8s)
    Men 200m→400m:     1.17s → 0.90s (−0.27s)
    Women 100m→200m:   0.48s → 0.37s (−0.11s)


Exactly 6 races vs all athlete-seasons
--------------------------------------
  Pairs more accurate: 9/10
  Pairs less accurate:  1/10
  Mean Δ (6 minus all): −1.904s
  Median Δ:             −0.264s

  Strongest signal in the entire analysis. Men's distance dominates:

Where accuracy improves most (by event group)
---------------------------------------------
  SPRINTS
    Modest but consistent gains at 4–6 races on adjacent pairs (100m↔200m).
    Typical improvement: 0.04–0.15s CV median |error|.
    Long hops (100m→400m) do NOT consistently improve — small samples, higher
    variance. Women 200m→400m and 400m→200m improve ~0.05–0.10s at 4 races.

  DISTANCE — Men (biggest signal)
    Athletes with 5–6 distance races show much tighter 800m / 1500m / 5000m
    relationships. Likely reflects more developed, consistent endurance athletes
    who race multiple times and refine fitness across the season.

  DISTANCE — Women
    Mixed at 4–5 races; insufficient n at 6 races for most pairs.
    Women's 800m→1500m and 1500m→800m can worsen at higher race counts in
  small samples — treat with caution.


================================================================================
PART 3 — PRACTICAL RECOMMENDATIONS
================================================================================

When projecting an unrun event
-------------------------------
  1. List the athlete's season PB times in the event group.
  2. Apply the best formula from Part 1 (or Part 2 if race-count context applies).
  3. Use CV median |error| as a typical ± band.
  4. Convert predicted time to WA in the TARGET event table.
  5. Compare WA margin vs nationals 8th-place threshold for that event.

Race-volume guidance
--------------------
  • Athletes with 5–6 season results in the group: models are meaningfully more
    trustworthy, especially men's 800m / 1500m / 5000m (−2 to −8s error reduction).
  • Athletes with exactly 4 races: do not assume better accuracy; wait for 5+.
  • Athletes with 1–3 results: use full-sample formulas but apply wider uncertainty.
  • Selection effect: high-race-count athletes may be more committed/coached —
    accuracy gains partly reflect athlete type, not just more data points.

Model selection quick reference
-------------------------------
  Two inputs bracketing target:
    Men 100m+200m→400m, Men 800m+1500m→5000m → multivariate OLS
    Women 800m+1500m→5000m → chain via 1500m

  One input, adjacent events:
  median_ratio or linear_ols (100m↔200m)

  One input, long hop:
    binned_ratio or median_ratio (100m→400m women; 200m→400m men)

  One input, inverse / endurance:
    robust_trimmed_linear or ratio_linear (400m→200m; 1500m→800m women)


================================================================================
PART 4 — CAVEATS
================================================================================

  • Cross-event WA comparison is invalid; always predict time first.
  • Pooled-athlete baseline (best mark per athlete across 2024–2026) differs
    slightly from athlete-season units used in the race-count analysis.
  • Race count = total discipline results in the event group, not per-event count.
  • Smaller samples at fixed race counts (especially 6 races, women) reduce
    model stability and limit which pairs can be evaluated.
  • Associative findings only; high-volume athletes may differ systematically
    from low-volume athletes beyond what race count alone explains.
