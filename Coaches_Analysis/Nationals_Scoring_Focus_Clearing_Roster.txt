Nationals Scoring Focus — Clearing-Capable Rosters
===============================================

Men's vs. Women's Teams — When Athletes Already Clear the Bar in Every Event

Based on 2024–2026 NIRCA outdoor data (RQ1B thresholds, RQ1C nationals-margin
comparisons, clearing-pool sizes). Relay-inclusive; March 1+ per season.

This report assumes a coach has many athletes in each event group who already
clear the 8th-place WA threshold for every event in that group. Clear rate is
no longer the decision driver. The question becomes: where do those athletes
have the largest margin above the 8th-place line, and where is the clearing
pool smallest?

Clearing the bar is necessary but not sufficient — only 8 score. Logic shifts to:

  1. RQ1C margin — which event gives the biggest buffer above the threshold
     among dual-event athletes?
  2. Field density — how many other clearing-capable athletes exist nationally
     (more clearers = harder to actually place)?
  3. Relay vs. individual — team WA can inflate relay marks; for clearing-
     capable athletes, check whether the relay still wins on margin, not just
     absolute WA.


================================================================================
MEN'S TEAM
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sprints
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Focus on: 400m (primary open), 4x100m (relay)

Rank | Event    | Why (for clearing-capable athletes)
-----|----------|--------------------------------------
1    | 400m     | Best open-sprint margin profile. Beats 200m (170–123) and
     |          | narrowly beats 100m (93–81) in nationals-margin head-to-heads.
     |          | Smallest clearing pool among open sprints (42 clearers vs.
     |          | 65–72 for 100m/200m) — less competition for top-8 spots.
2    | 4x100m   | Wins every sprint margin comparison vs. open events (e.g. vs.
     |          | 100m 209–49, vs. 200m 188–60). Best relay option for clearing-
     |          | capable sprinters. Tradeoff: largest clearing pool nationally
     |          | (133) — very crowded at Nationals.
3    | 100m     | Beats 200m on margin (243–204). Use for athletes whose 100m
     |          | margin clearly exceeds their 400m margin.
     | Avoid:   | 200m, 4x400m as primary. 200m loses margin comparisons to both
     |          | 100m and 400m. 4x400 beats 400m on margin (172–87) but has a
     |          | much larger clearing pool (81) and only modest margin gains —
     |          | better as depth/second relay than default focus for elite open
     |          | sprinters.

Coaching read: Spread clearing-capable sprinters — 400m and 100m for individual
points, one 4x100 from your best legs. Don't stack everyone into 4x100; margin
is best there but the field is deepest.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Distance
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Focus on: Steeplechase (if cleared), then 5000m, then 1500m

Rank | Event              | Why
-----|--------------------|------------------------------------------------------
1    | 3000m Steeplechase | Dominates nationals-margin comparisons vs. every
     |                    | other distance event (vs. 1500m 128–39, vs. 5000m
     |                    | 76–30, vs. 800m 85–32). Smallest clearing pool (24).
     |                    | Lowest bar — clearing-capable steeplers often have
     |                    | the largest margin in the group.
2    | 5000m              | Beats 1500m (225–209) and 800m (140–79) on margin.
     |                    | Beats 4x400 (81–76) among dual athletes. Bar ~786 vs.
     |                    | 827–829 for 800/1500 — more room above the line.
3    | 1500m              | Beats 800m clearly (356–225). Default for milers who
     |                    | aren't steeple/5K types. Modal best-event label for
     |                    | distance men.
     | Secondary:         | 4x100m / 4x400m — for clearing distance athletes,
     |                    | relays usually lose margin comparisons to steeple and
     |                    | often to 5000m. Use relays to fill team slots, not as
     |                    | primary focus for best individuals.
     | Avoid:             | 800m — loses margin comparisons to 1500m, 5000m, and
     |                    | steeple. Hardest nationals bar (829 WA). Worst
     |                    | individual bet even for clearing-capable athletes.

Coaching read: Assign clearing-capable distance athletes by specialty margin,
not roster size. Steeple → 5K → 1500m hierarchy. Keep 800m only when clearly
above the line and stronger there than at 1500m.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hurdles
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Focus on: 400m Hurdles (primary individual), 4x100m (relay)

Rank | Event        | Why
-----|--------------|----------------------------------------------------------
1    | 400m Hurdles | Beats 110mH on nationals margin (42–27). Among clearing
     |              | hurdlers, long hurdles are the stronger individual bet.
     |              | Smaller clearing pool (27 vs. 38 for 110mH).
2    | 110m Hurdles | Best for dedicated sprint hurdlers who don't run 400mH.
     |              | Margin vs. relays is essentially even (21–21 vs. 4x400;
     |              | 18–21 vs. 4x100).
3    | 4x100m       | Pecking order and margin favor it over 4x400 for hurdlers
     |              | with speed. Good relay assignment for clearing-capable
     |              | athletes.
     | Avoid:       | 4x400m as primary — often tied or barely ahead on margin
     |              | vs. hurdle events, with a large clearing pool (81). Depth
     |              | relay, not the focus for best hurdlers.

Coaching read: Clearing-capable men hurdlers should default to 400mH unless
clearly a 110m specialist. Relay them in 4x100, not automatically 4x400.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Jumps
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Focus on: Triple Jump, then High Jump

Rank | Event       | Why
-----|-------------|-----------------------------------------------------------
1    | Triple Jump | Wins margin comparisons vs. long jump (61–19) and high jump
     |             | (21–11). Lower bar than long jump (726 vs. 792 WA).
     |             | Clearing-capable triple jumpers typically have the most
     |             | buffer.
2    | High Jump   | Beats long jump on margin (34–20). Beats 4x400 (14–10).
     |             | Strong second choice for athletes who don't triple.
3    | 4x100m      | Only if the athlete has real sprint value; long jump
     |             | specialists lose margin to 4x100 (46–29).
     | Avoid:      | Long Jump — most clearers (34) but loses every margin
     |             | comparison within the jump group and has the highest bar.
     |             | Worst focus for clearing-capable jumpers who also clear
     |             | triple/high.

Coaching read: If they clear all three jumps, triple first, high second, long
only if it's clearly their outlier mark.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Throws
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Focus on: Shot Put, then Discus

Rank | Event    | Why
-----|----------|-----------------------------------------------------------
1    | Shot Put | Beats discus on nationals margin among dual throwers (67–45).
     |          | Beats 4x100 (20–5) and splits evenly with 4x400 when throwers
     |          | leg relays.
2    | Discus   | Strong margin vs. relays (13–2 vs. 4x100). Secondary for dual
     |          | throwers.
     | Avoid:   | Javelin / Hammer — no clearing athletes in the dataset.

Coaching read: Clearing-capable throwers should stay in the circle. Shot put is
the default individual focus; discus for dual-competency athletes where margin
favors disc.


================================================================================
WOMEN'S TEAM
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sprints
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Focus on: 400m (primary open), 4x100m (relay)

Rank | Event    | Why
-----|----------|-----------------------------------------------------------
1    | 400m     | Beats 100m (36–22) and crushes 200m (102–25) on nationals
     |          | margin. Best open sprint for clearing-capable women. Smallest
     |          | open clearing pool among 100/200/400 (23 clearers).
2    | 4x100m   | Wins all individual×relay margin comparisons (e.g. vs. 100m
     |          | 124–12, vs. 400m 48–15). Best relay focus. Crowded field
     |          | (105 clearers) — spread talent, don't concentrate everyone here.
3    | 100m     | Beats 200m on margin (157–46). For athletes whose 100m margin
     |          | exceeds 400m.
     | Secondary:| 4x400m — more viable than for men, but loses to 4x100 on
     |          | margin (67–41). Second relay, not primary.
     | Avoid:   | 200m — loses margin to both 100m and 400m. Weakest open sprint
     |          | for clearing-capable athletes.

Coaching read: Women's clearing-capable sprinters → 400m individually, 4x100 as
the A relay. Open 100m/200m only when margin data clearly supports it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Distance
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Focus on: Steeplechase, then 5000m, then 4x400m / 1500m

Rank | Event              | Why
-----|--------------------|------------------------------------------------------
1    | 3000m Steeplechase | Overwhelming margin advantage vs. 1500m (75–6), 5000m
     |                    | (40–4), and 800m (45–7). Beats 4x400 (28–25). Lowest bar
     |                    | in the group (~511 WA).
2    | 5000m              | Beats 1500m (79–65) and 800m (58–27). Beats 4x400
     |                    | (42–29). Best individual target for non-steeple distance
     |                    | runners.
3    | 4x400m             | Beats 1500m on margin (74–58) among dual athletes. Viable
     |                    | relay for clearing milers who aren't 5K/steeple types.
4    | 1500m              | Beats 800m (178–90). Default for milers who don't have
     |                    | stronger margins at 5K, steeple, or 4x400.
     | Avoid:             | 800m — loses margin to 1500m, 5000m, and steeple. Weakest
     |                    | individual distance bet.

Coaching read: Steeple → 5K → 4x400/1500m hierarchy. Don't default milers to
800m even when they clear it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hurdles
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Focus on: 400m Hurdles

Rank | Event        | Why
-----|--------------|----------------------------------------------------------
1    | 400m Hurdles | Best margin in the group: beats 100mH (26–18), beats 4x400
     |              | strongly (25–9), beats 4x100 (17–13). Highest clear rate
     |              | among women's hurdle events (40%).
2    | 100m Hurdles | For sprint-hurdle specialists. Loses to 400mH on margin but
     |              | still clearing-viable.
3    | 4x100m       | Beats 100mH on margin (25–21). Relay option for hurdlers with
     |              | speed.
     | Avoid:       | 4x400m as primary — loses margin to 400mH (9–25). Not the
     |              | focus for best clearing hurdlers.

Coaching read: Clearing-capable women hurdlers should almost all default to
400mH unless exclusively a 100mH athlete.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Jumps
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Focus on: High Jump, then Triple Jump

Rank | Event       | Why
-----|-------------|-----------------------------------------------------------
1    | High Jump   | Best margin among women's jumps: beats long jump (24–15) and
     |             | beats triple jump (12–6). Lowest bar (~681 WA).
2    | Triple Jump | Beats long jump (33–13) and 4x400 (10–2). Second choice for
     |             | dual horizontal/high jump athletes.
3    | 4x100m      | Margin roughly even with high jump (13–14). Only for fast
     |             | jumpers where relay adds team value.
     | Avoid:      | Long Jump — loses margin to both high jump and triple. Highest
     |             | bar among jumps (~760 WA).

Coaching read: Unlike men (triple first), women's clearing-capable jumpers should
lead with high jump, then triple.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Throws
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Focus on: Discus, then Shot Put

Rank | Event    | Why
-----|----------|-----------------------------------------------------------
1    | Discus   | Beats shot on margin (41–32). Beats 4x400 (6–1). Lowest bar in
     |          | women's track (~504 WA) — clearing-capable discus throwers often
     |          | have the largest margin in the group.
2    | Shot Put | Secondary for dual throwers. Still viable (20% population clear
     |          | rate).
     | Avoid:   | Javelin / Hammer — zero clearers in dataset.


================================================================================
CROSS-CUTTING STRATEGY — MANY CLEARING-CAPABLE ATHLETES
================================================================================

Clear rate no longer drives placement — margins and field density do.

Principle              | Application
-----------------------|--------------------------------------------------------
Spread, don't stack    | Many clearing athletes in one event (especially 4x100,
                       | 1500m) means internal competition for 8 spots. Assign by
                       | margin strength across events.
Margin > best WA       | 4x400 often shows highest WA but weaker conversion; for
                       | clearing athletes, use RQ1C margin head-to-heads instead.
Smaller pools = edge   | Steeple, 400m (women sprints), 400mH, triple (men), high
                       | jump (women), discus (women) have fewer clearing
                       | competitors.
Relays are team slots  | Staff 4x100 from clearing sprinters/hurdlers; don't let
                       | relay WA pull distance/field specialists off their best
                       | individual margin event.
Season structure       | Still build volume early and sharpen late (~half peak in
                       | final 25% of schedule). Event choice changes; timing doesn't.


QUICK REFERENCE — CLEARING-CAPABLE ATHLETE FOCUS
-------------------------------------------------

Group     | Men — focus here              | Women — focus here
----------|-------------------------------|----------------------------------
Sprints   | 400m, 4x100                   | 400m, 4x100
Distance  | Steeple → 5000m → 1500m       | Steeple → 5000m → 4x400/1500m
Hurdles   | 400mH, 4x100                  | 400mH
Jumps     | Triple → High                 | High → Triple
Throws    | Shot → Discus                 | Discus → Shot


SHIFT FROM CLEAR-RATE REPORT
----------------------------

When everyone clears, stop chasing accessible bars and start chasing where your
athletes are furthest above the line with the fewest rivals — and spread them so
your team isn't fighting itself for the same eight places.

See also: Coaches_Analysis/Nationals_Scoring_Focus_Men_Women.txt (clear-rate
framing for rosters still building toward the nationals bar).

Source: RQ1B nationals 8th-place thresholds, RQ1C nationals-margin head-to-
heads, clearing-pool counts — relay-inclusive outdoor data 2024–2026 (March 1+).
