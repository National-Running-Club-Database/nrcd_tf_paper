Nationals Scoring Focus — Men's vs. Women's Teams
=================================================

Based on 2024–2026 NIRCA outdoor data: 8th-place WA thresholds (RQ1B), how many
athletes clear that bar (RQ1C clear count + clear rate), and head-to-head
nationals-margin comparisons. Goal: maximize athletes finishing 8th or higher
at Nationals.

How to read this: "Clear count" = how many athletes in the full dataset cleared
the bar (team depth signal). "Clear rate" = share of athletes who competed in
that event who cleared (conversion signal). Both matter — a high count with a
low rate means a big pool but tough conversion; a high rate with a small pool
means a niche opportunity.


================================================================================
MEN'S TEAM
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sprints
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Priority | Event          | 8th-place bar | Clear rate | Clears
---------|----------------|---------------|------------|-------
1        | 4x100m         | 760 WA        | 26.3%      | 133
2        | 200m           | 820 WA        | 10.2%      | 72
3        | 4x400m         | 785 WA        | 9.9%       | 81
         | Deprioritize: 100m (818 WA, 9.3%, 65), 400m (792 WA, 6.9%, 42)

Justification:
  • 4x100m is the single best scoring pathway for men: most clearers in the
    entire population (133), highest clear rate among sprint events (26.3%), and
    a bar ~58 WA points lower than open 100m. Among athletes who do both,
    4x100 wins the nationals-margin comparison 209–49 over 100m.
  • 200m is the best open sprint for individual points: 72 clearers (more than
    100m's 65) with a similar high bar (~820). Use it for athletes who aren't
    on the A 4x100 but have 200m speed.
  • 4x400m produces 81 clearers (second-most overall) but only 9.9% of 4x400
    athletes clear — many leg it without being true scoring threats. Staff it
    for depth and a second relay score, not as the primary sprint strategy.
  • Avoid loading up on 100m and 400m for scoring: highest or near-highest
    bars with the worst clear rates in the sprint group.

Season structure: 6–10 race results; volume helps (~+8 WA per extra race).
~50% of multi-meet sprinters still peak in the final 25% of their schedule —
build volume early, sharpen late.

Distance
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Priority | Event                    | 8th-place bar | Clear rate | Clears
---------|--------------------------|---------------|------------|-------
1        | 4x100m (relay crossover) | 760 WA        | 26.3%      | 133
2        | 1500m                    | 827 WA        | 4.3%       | 51
3        | 3000m Steeplechase       | 456 WA        | 9.1%       | 24
4        | 5000m                    | 786 WA        | 4.9%       | 36
         | Secondary: 4x400m (785 WA, 9.9%, 81)
         | Deprioritize: 800m (829 WA — hardest bar, 4.6%, 46)

Justification:
  • Distance rosters are large, but individual clear rates are low (4–5%) for
    800m, 1500m, and 5000m. Relay crossover is the most efficient way to get
    distance athletes on the scoring sheet: 4x100 again leads on conversion.
  • 1500m gets priority among individual distance events because it has the
    most clearers (51) and beats 800m in nationals-margin head-to-heads
    (356–225 among dual athletes). It's the default primary for milers even
    though the bar is high (~827).
  • Steeplechase has by far the lowest bar (456 WA) — roughly 370 points below
    800m. Only 24 clearers, but 9.1% clear rate with a tiny specialist pool.
    If you have anyone who can run steeple, this is high ROI.
  • 5000m beats 1500m in nationals-margin comparisons among dual athletes
    (225–209) and has a lower bar than 1500m or 800m (~786). Assign true 5K
    types here rather than defaulting everyone to the mile.
  • 800m is the worst scoring bet for men: highest nationals bar in the sport
    (829 WA) with only 4.6% clearing. Avoid steering athletes here unless
    they're already clearly above the line.

Season structure: Highest volume tolerance of any group — aim for 7–9+ results;
~+12 WA per extra race in fixed-effects models. Late peaking still common
(~47% in final 25%).

Hurdles
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Priority | Event          | 8th-place bar | Clear rate | Clears
---------|----------------|---------------|------------|-------
1        | 110m Hurdles   | 629 WA        | 32.5%      | 38
2        | 400m Hurdles   | 728 WA        | 16.8%      | 27
3        | 4x100m         | 760 WA        | 26.3%      | 133
         | Deprioritize: 4x400m as primary hurdle path (785 WA, 9.9%, 81)

Justification:
  • 110m Hurdles is the best individual scoring event for men's hurdlers:
    highest clear rate of any men's event in the dataset (32.5%) and the bar
    is ~200 WA below open sprints. Among 110mH athletes, 38 clear — comparable
    to 800m/1500m despite a much smaller entry pool.
  • 400m Hurdles is a solid secondary (16.8% clear rate; beats 110mH in
    head-to-head margin 42–27 among dual hurdlers) for long-hurdle types.
  • 4x100 is worth staffing hurdlers on — speed + hurdle skill transfers —
    but 110mH should remain the primary individual scoring target.
  • Don't treat 4x400 as the main hurdle scoring plan: many hurdlers leg it
    (688 list it as "best event"), but only 9.9% clear the nationals bar.
    Relay WA inflates marks without converting to top-8 finishes.

Season structure: 4–6 focused race results; men's hurdles show weak
volume→improvement signal. Quality and late sharpness over meet quantity.

Jumps
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Priority | Event      | 8th-place bar | Clear rate | Clears
---------|------------|---------------|------------|-------
1        | High Jump  | 732 WA        | 27.9%      | 29
2        | Triple Jump| 725 WA        | 23.0%      | 23
3        | Long Jump  | 792 WA        | 13.6%      | 34
         | Secondary: 4x100m (760 WA, 26.3%, 133)

Justification:
  • High Jump has the best clear rate among men's jumps (27.9%) with an
    accessible bar (~732). More men clear high jump than triple (29 vs. 23)
    despite fewer total competitors.
  • Triple Jump beats long jump in nationals-margin head-to-heads (61–19 among
    dual athletes) and has a much lower bar (726 vs. 792). Prioritize triple
    over long for athletes who can do both.
  • Long Jump has the most clearers (34) but the worst clear rate (13.6%) and
    the highest bar of the three — it's a depth event, not the best conversion
    bet.
  • 4x100 for jumpers who have speed is a secondary team play; among dual
    athletes, 4x100 usually wins over long jump on margin, but jumpers who
    clear their primary event should stay there.

Season structure: 3–5 meet results; more races don't reliably help men jumpers.
Technical consistency over volume.

Throws
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Priority | Event    | 8th-place bar | Clear rate | Clears
---------|----------|---------------|------------|-------
1        | Discus   | 659 WA        | 19.1%      | 25
2        | Shot Put | 672 WA        | 12.8%      | 22
         | Deprioritize: Javelin, Hammer (0% clear rate, 0 clearers)

Justification:
  • Discus has the most clearers (25), the best clear rate (19.1%), and the
    lowest bar (~659). Among dual throwers, discus beats shot in nationals-
    margin comparisons (though shot wins overall head-to-head 67–45 — assign
    by individual margin vs. bar).
  • Shot Put is secondary: slightly higher bar, fewer clearers, lower rate.
  • When throwers also leg relays, their primary throw beats 4x100 in margin
    comparisons (discus 13–2, shot 20–5) — keep them focused on the circle.
  • Do not target javelin or hammer for nationals scoring in this dataset:
    zero athletes cleared the bar across three years.

Season structure: 3–5 results; men's throws show no meaningful
volume→improvement link.


Men's Team — Overall Scoring Portfolio (ranked)
------------------------------------------------
  1. 4x100m — best event for maximizing scorers
  2. 110m Hurdles — best individual clear rate (32.5%)
  3. 200m — best open sprint for individual points
  4. High Jump — best jump clear rate (27.9%)
  5. 4x400m — depth/second relay, not primary strategy
  6. 1500m / Steeple / Discus — specialist paths with clear justification

Avoid steering the roster toward: 800m, open 100m, long jump, 4x400 as a
default "best event."

WOMEN'S TEAM
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sprints
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Priority | Event          | 8th-place bar | Clear rate | Clears
---------|----------------|---------------|------------|-------
1        | 4x100m         | 776 WA        | 37.2%      | 105
2        | 4x400m         | 747 WA        | 18.0%      | 78
         | Deprioritize: 100m (814 WA, 9.3%, 27), 200m (799 WA, 7.2%, 25),
                       400m (788 WA, 9.4%, 23)

Justification:
  • 4x100m dominates women's sprint scoring: 105 clearers (most in the women's
    dataset) and 37.2% clear rate — the highest conversion rate of any women's
    event. Bar (~776) is ~38 WA below open 100m (~814). Among dual athletes,
    4x100 beats 100m 124–12 on nationals margin.
  • 4x400m is a legitimate second relay target for women (18.0% clear vs. 9.9%
    for men; 78 clearers). Still well behind 4x100 on conversion, but much
    more viable than on the men's side.
  • Open sprints (100m, 200m, 400m) are poor scoring bets: bars among the
    highest in women's track (788–814 WA) with clear rates of only 7–9%. Use
    open events only for athletes already above the line — not as the default
    team strategy.
  • Among open sprints, 100m beats 200m in margin (157–46) if you must pick
    one individual event.

Season structure: 6–10 results; ~+9 WA per extra race. ~50% late-peak rate
among multi-meet athletes.

Distance

Priority | Event                    | 8th-place bar | Clear rate | Clears
---------|--------------------------|---------------|------------|-------
1        | 4x100m (relay crossover) | 776 WA        | 37.2%      | 105
2        | 1500m                    | 792 WA        | 8.0%       | 46
3        | 3000m Steeplechase       | 511 WA        | 16.3%      | 22
4        | 5000m                    | 808 WA        | 9.6%       | 29
         | Secondary: 4x400m (748 WA, 18.0%, 78)
         | Deprioritize: 800m (760 WA, 6.4%, 30)

Justification:
  • 4x100 again leads for getting distance athletes on the score sheet.
  • 1500m is the top individual distance scoring event for women: 46 clearers
    (third-most among all women's events) and beats 800m in margin (178–90).
    Default primary for milers.
  • Steeplechase is a strong niche: bar only 511 WA (lowest among women's
    distance events), 16.3% clear rate — second-best rate in the distance
    group. Beats 1500m and 5000m in head-to-head margin overwhelmingly among
    dual athletes.
  • 5000m beats 1500m in margin among dual athletes (79–65) but has a very
    high bar (808.5) — only assign true 5K specialists. Steeple beats 5000m
    40–4 among dual athletes.
  • 800m underperforms: 6.4% clear rate despite a moderate bar. 1500m and
    steeple are better targets.

Season structure: 7–9+ results; strongest volume response in women's track
(~+17 WA per extra race).

Hurdles
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Priority | Event          | 8th-place bar | Clear rate | Clears
---------|----------------|---------------|------------|-------
1        | 400m Hurdles   | 661 WA        | 40.0%      | 32
2        | 100m Hurdles   | 608 WA        | 27.2%      | 28
3        | 4x100m         | 776 WA        | 37.2%      | 105
         | Deprioritize: 4x400m as primary (748 WA, 18.0%, 78)

Justification:
  • 400m Hurdles is the single best women's hurdle scoring event: 40.0% clear
    rate (highest of any women's hurdle/sprint event in the dataset) with a bar
    ~150 WA below open 400m. Beats 4x400 in margin 25–9 among dual athletes.
  • 100m Hurdles is an excellent secondary: bar only 608 WA, 27.2% clear rate.
    Slightly fewer clearers than 400mH but very accessible.
  • 4x100 for hurdlers with speed is viable, but 400mH should be the primary
    individual target for long hurdlers; 100mH for sprint hurdlers.
  • 400mH beats 100mH in margin among dual hurdlers (26–18) — when an athlete
    can do both, lean 400mH.

Season structure: 4–6 focused results; women's hurdles show strong volume
response (~+22 WA per race) — more than men's hurdles.

Jumps

Priority | Event      | 8th-place bar | Clear rate | Clears
---------|------------|---------------|------------|-------
1        | High Jump  | 681 WA        | 37.3%      | 28
2        | Triple Jump| 741 WA        | 31.6%      | 18
3        | Long Jump  | 760 WA        | 16.6%      | 26
         | Secondary: 4x100m (776 WA, 37.2%, 105)

Justification:
  • High Jump is the top women's jump for scoring: 37.3% clear rate (tied with

    jumps.
  • Long Jump has more clearers (26) than triple (18) but a much worse clear
    rate (16.6%) and higher bar — deprioritize unless it's clearly the
    athlete's better margin event.
  • 4x100 is a secondary option for fast jumpers, but high jump and triple
    jump specialists should stay in their primary event for individual points.

Season structure: 3–5 results; women jumpers show modest volume benefit
(~+7 WA per race).

================================================================================
KEY GENDER DIFFERENCES
================================================================================

Topic              | Men                              | Women
-------------------|----------------------------------|----------------------------------
Best relay         | 4x100 first; 4x400 depth only    | 4x100 first; 4x400 more viable (18% vs 10%)
Best open sprint   | 200m (72 clearers)               | Open sprints weak — stay in relays
Best hurdle score  | 110mH (32.5% clear)              | 400mH (40.0% clear)
Best jump score    | High Jump (27.9%)                | High Jump (37.3%)
Best distance score| 1500m depth + steeple niche      | 1500m depth + steeple niche
Hardest to score   | 800m (829 bar)                   | 100m / 5000m (814 / 809 bar)
Volume matters most| Distance (+12 WA/race)           | Distance (+17 WA/race)


Source: RQ1B nationals 8th-place thresholds, RQ1C clear counts/rates, pairwise
margin comparisons — relay-inclusive outdoor data 2024–2026 (March 1+). Relay
WA credited to leg athletes; only 4x100m and 4x400m have valid relay WA scoring.
