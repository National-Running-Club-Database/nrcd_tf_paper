Major Trends in Relay-Inclusive Track & Field Analysis (2024–2026)
================================================================
Outdoor track, results on or after March 1 each year. Relay team World Athletics
(WA) points are credited to each leg athlete; an athlete's relay best is the
maximum across relay appearances.

Source outputs: RQ1A (best event), RQ1B (nationals 8th-place thresholds),
RQ1C (competitiveness vs. nationals bar) across Sprinters, Distance, Hurdles,
Jumps, and Throws folders in Relays_Findings/.


1. DATA SCOPE AND LIMITATIONS
-----------------------------
Only 4x100m and 4x400m have nonzero WA scoring in the relay dataset. Other
relay types (4x200m, 4x800m, DMR, SMR, Swedish Relay) are excluded from
threshold-based comparisons because nationals 8th-place WA scores are missing
or zero. Findings below therefore reflect these two sprint relays only.


2. RELAYS DRAMATICALLY SHIFT "BEST EVENT" (RQ1A)
------------------------------------------------
When relays are included, 4x400m becomes the modal best event across nearly
every discipline and gender — often by a wide margin.

Examples (athletes whose highest WA mark is in that event):
  Men sprinters:   4x400m 611 | 100m 351 | 4x100m 308
  Women sprinters: 4x400m 322 | 4x100m 216 | 100m 142
  Men hurdles:     4x400m 688 | 4x100m 395 | 400m Hurdles 95
  Men jumps:       4x400m 699 | 4x100m 372 | Long Jump 155
  Men throws:      4x400m 707 | 4x100m 394 | Shot Put 113
  Women jumps:     4x400m 354 | 4x100m 221 | Long Jump 87

Distance is the main exception on RQ1A: men's 1500m (723) and 800m (487) still
outrank 4x400m (475) for best-event counts, though relays remain substantial.

Trend: Team relay WA scores are often higher than individual marks, so many
athletes appear "best" at a relay even when their specialty is an individual
event.


3. NATIONALS BARS ARE LOWER FOR RELAYS (RQ1B)
-----------------------------------------------
Three-year average 8th-place nationals WA thresholds (2024–2026):

  Men:   800m 829.3 and 1500m 827.0 (highest) … 4x400m 785.3 … 4x100m 760.3
  Women: 100m 814.3 and 5000m 808.5 (highest) … 4x100m 776.0 … 4x400m 747.7

Relays sit in the middle-to-lower range of nationals competitiveness. The
4x100m bar is especially accessible relative to open sprints (e.g., men 100m
818.0 vs. 4x100m 760.3; women 100m 814.3 vs. 4x100m 776.0).


4. BEST EVENT ≠ MOST COMPETITIVE EVENT (RQ1C)
----------------------------------------------
RQ1C asks which event gives the largest margin above (or smallest margin
below) the nationals 8th-place bar — a different question than highest
absolute WA (RQ1A).

Key pattern — 4x400m dominates RQ1C athlete counts but has low clear-rates:
  Men sprints:   RQ1C modal event 4x400m (614 athletes), but only 9.9% clear bar
  Women sprints: RQ1C modal event 4x400m (345 athletes), 18.0% clear bar

Meanwhile 4x100m has the highest nationals clear-rates in the population:
  Men:   133/506 clear (26.3%) — highest count of any event
  Women: 105/282 clear (37.2%) — highest count of any event

So many athletes are "most competitive" at 4x400m by margin, yet far fewer
actually clear the nationals threshold there than in 4x100m. High team WA on
relays inflates best-event labels without matching nationals-level access.

RQ1A vs. RQ1C agreement (same athlete, same discipline):
  Highest: Men throws 91.5%, Women sprints 91.0%, Men sprints 89.8%
  Lowest:  Women distance 75.8%, Women throws 76.5%, Women hurdles 77.3%

Distance shows the most RQ1A/RQ1C disagreement — individual middle-distance
events (1500m, 5000m) remain top RQ1C choices even when relays dominate
best-event counts for other groups.


5. INDIVIDUAL VS. RELAY HEAD-TO-HEAD (RQ1C MARGINS)
-----------------------------------------------------
Among athletes with marks in both an individual event and a relay, relays
usually win the competitiveness comparison (larger margin vs. threshold).

Strong relay advantage (examples):
  Men 100m vs. 4x100m (258 dual athletes): 4x100m wins 209 | 100m wins 49
  Men 200m vs. 4x100m (248):               4x100m wins 188 | 200m wins 60
  Women 100m vs. 4x100m (136):             4x100m wins 124 | 100m wins 12

Distance retains more individual strength:
  Men 5000m vs. 4x400m (157): 5000m wins 81 | 4x400m wins 76 (near split)
  Men 3000m SC vs. 4x400m (86): steeple wins 46 | 4x400m wins 40

Throws can favor individuals when overlap is small:
  Men shot put vs. 4x100m (25): shot put wins 20 | 4x100m wins 5
  Men discus vs. 4x100m (15):   discus wins 13 | 4x100m wins 2

Hurdles are mixed at small sample sizes; women's 400m Hurdles vs. 4x400m
(35 dual): 400mH wins 25 | 4x400m wins 9.


6. PAIRWISE BEST-EVENT: INDIVIDUAL VS. RELAY (RQ1A)
---------------------------------------------------
Across all disciplines, for every individual×relay pairing among athletes
competing in both events, relay events "win" as best event far more often
than individual events (roughly 48 of 63 pair types favor relays vs. 15
favoring individuals).

By discipline (individual wins / total pair types):
  Distance: 6/16 — strongest for individuals (1500m, 5000m vs. relays)
  Throws:   5/15
  Jumps:    3/12
  Sprinters: 1/12 (men's 200m vs. 4x400m only)
  Hurdles:  0/8 — relays always win pairwise best-event comparisons


7. MULTI-EVENT PARTICIPATION IS COMMON
--------------------------------------
Roughly half of athletes in each discipline compete in 2+ scored events
(e.g., men sprints 910/1830; men distance 1283/2518). Relay inclusion
amplifies this: 822 men and 434 women have 4x400m results, spreading relay
exposure across sprint, distance, hurdle, jump, and throw rosters.


8. POPULATION-LEVEL NATIONALS ACCESS
------------------------------------
When ranked by count of athletes clearing the 8th-place bar (not rate alone):

Men — top events by clear count:
  1. 4x100m (133)
  2. 4x400m (81)
  3. 200m (72)
  4. 100m (65)
  … middle/long distance events: 46–51 clears despite large entry pools

Women — top events by clear count:
  1. 4x100m (105)
  2. 4x400m (78)
  3. 1500m (46)
  … several field/hurdle events show high clear-rates at smaller N

Relays provide the single largest pathway by headcount for men approaching
nationals-level WA scores; for women, 4x100m leads clearly.


9. CROSS-DISCIPLINE RELAY PENETRATION
-------------------------------------
Because relay CSVs are shared across discipline folders, hurdle, jump, and
throw athletes appear heavily in 4x400m and 4x100m RQ1C rankings even though
their primary discipline is not sprints. Example — men hurdles RQ1C: 672
athletes "most competitive" at 4x400m vs. 86 at 400m Hurdles. This reflects
roster crossover (many distance/hurdle/field athletes leg relays), not that
hurdles specialists should pivot to relays.


10. SYNTHESIS — PRACTICAL TAKEAWAYS
-----------------------------------
a) Including relays changes the story of athlete specialization: 4x400m
   becomes the dominant "best event" label across the sport, driven by team
   WA inflation rather than individual open-event performance.

b) For nationals competitiveness, 4x100m is the stronger relay target
   (higher clear-rates, lower relative bar) than 4x400m, despite 4x400m
   winning more RQ1C margin comparisons.

c) Middle- and long-distance individual events remain the best individual
   alternatives to relays; distance athletes are the group most likely to
   have an individual event as both best event and most competitive event.

d) Field events and hurdles show high per-athlete clear-rates at nationals
   thresholds for their primary events, but small overlap with relay rosters
   limits relay-vs-individual comparisons; where overlap exists, relays
   often still win on WA margin.

e) Analysis is incomplete for distance relays (4x800m) and specialty relays
   until WA scoring is populated; current conclusions apply to sprint relays
   only.

f) Coaches and athletes should treat RQ1A (best WA) and RQ1C (vs. nationals
   bar) as complementary: high relay WA may mark an athlete's "best" result
   while an individual event may still be the more realistic nationals path.
