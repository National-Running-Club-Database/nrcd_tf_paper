Impact of Relay Inclusion on RQ1A, RQ1B, and RQ1C
==================================================
Comparison of non_relays_findings/ (individual events only) vs.
relays_findings/ (individual + relay events, team WA credited to each leg).

Scope: Outdoor track 2024–2026 combined, results on or after March 1.
Relay scoring: full team World Athletics points assigned to every leg athlete;
athlete relay best = maximum across relay appearances.

Only 4x100m and 4x400m have nonzero WA in the relay dataset. Other relay types
(4x200m, 4x800m, DMR, SMR, Swedish Relay) appear in data but are excluded from
RQ1B/RQ1C threshold comparisons.


======================================================================
OVERVIEW — WHAT CHANGES WHEN RELAYS ARE INCLUDED
======================================================================

| RQ   | Question (short)                         | Main effect of relay inclusion |
|------|------------------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| RQ1A | Which event is the athlete best at?      | **Largest change.** 4x400m becomes modal best event in most disciplines; individual-event best-event counts fall sharply. |
| RQ1B | Nationals top-8 score distributions?     | **Additive.** Two new events (4x100m, 4x400m) with their own thresholds; all individual-event thresholds unchanged. |
| RQ1C | Which event maximizes nationals margin?  | **Major reframing.** Relays become the modal RQ1C recommendation in sprints/hurdles/jumps/throws; 4x100m leads population clear counts; RQ1A–RQ1C agreement rates shift by discipline. |


======================================================================
RQ1A — WHICH EVENT ARE ATHLETES RELATIVELY BEST AT?
======================================================================

### Methodological change
- Event pool expands from discipline-specific individual events to those events
  **plus** all standard relays (scored: 4x100m, 4x400m only).
- Pairwise comparisons add every individual×relay combination (e.g., 100m vs.
  4x100m, 1500m vs. 4x400m), not just individual×individual pairs.

### Change 1: Modal best event flips to 4x400m (most disciplines)

Without relays, the modal best event is always an individual event. With relays,
4x400m is the top best-event label in every discipline except men's and women's
distance (where 1500m remains #1).

### Change 2: Pairwise best-event landscape is transformed
- **New pair types:** Every individual event is compared against 4x100m and
  4x400m (full cross-product), adding relay-inclusive histograms not present
  in the non-relay analysis.
- **Relay wins overwhelmingly:** Across all individual×relay pairings among
  athletes in both events, relay events are the pairwise “best event” in ~48 of
  63 pair types vs. ~15 for individual events.
- **Distance is the exception:** 6 of 16 distance pair types still favor
  individual events (notably 1500m and 5000m vs. relays).
- **Hurdles:** 0 of 8 individual×relay pair types favor the individual event.

### Change 3: Interpretation of “specialization” shifts
Without relays, RQ1A describes **individual event specialization** (e.g.,
men sprinters best at 100m, men distance at 1500m, men jumps at long jump).
With relays, the same metric often measures **where team relay WA inflates
personal profiles** — many field and hurdle athletes appear “best” at 4x400m
because they leg relays, not because they specialize in the 400m.

### What does NOT change in RQ1A
- Tie-breaking and season filter (March 1+) logic are the same.
- Among athletes restricted to individual events only, relative ordering of
  individual best-event counts is preserved (same rank order, lower absolute
  counts because some athletes’ global best moves to a relay).

RQ1B — NATIONALS TOP-8 SCORE DISTRIBUTIONS
======================================================================

### Methodological change
- Relay finals/unlabeled rounds are added to the nationals top-8 extraction.
- 100m/200m continue to use Prelims where applicable; relays use Finals.
- Relay rows are loaded once from the Sprinters relay CSV to avoid duplicates
  across discipline folders.

### Change 1: Two new events in the threshold table
Individual-event 8th-place thresholds are **unchanged** (same 3-year averages).
Relays are **inserted** into the ranked list:

### Change 2: Relay thresholds are mid-pack, not elite-tier
- Men 4x400m (785.3) sits just below 5000m; men 4x100m (760.3) is more
  accessible than open 100m (818.0).
- Women 4x100m (776.0) is below open 100m (814.3); women 4x400m (747.7) is
  among the lower middle-distance/relay bars.

### Change 3: Events still excluded from RQ1B/RQ1C
4x800m, 4x200m, DMR, SMR, and Swedish Relay have rows in relay CSVs but
**zero WA points**, so no nationals threshold can be computed. This is a data
gap, not a methodological choice.

### What does NOT change in RQ1B
- Top-8 extraction rules for individual events.
- Per-season distributions and individual-event 8th-place values.
- RQ1B answers for individual events are directly comparable across analyses.

RQ1C — WHICH EVENT SHOULD ATHLETES PURSUE TO BE MOST COMPETITIVE?
======================================================================

### Methodological change
- Athlete personal bests now include relay WA (team score per leg).
- Threshold set expands with 4x100m and 4x400m from relay-inclusive RQ1B.
- New comparison block: individual×relay margin head-to-heads.
- Athlete pools grow because relay legs add scored events (and, in hurdle/jump/
  throw folders, many relay-only participants enter the discipline sample).

Lower agreement in men's sprints reflects athletes whose best WA is a relay
but whose best nationals margin remains an individual sprint (or vice versa).

### Change 4: Nationals clear-rates — 4x100m vs. 4x400m paradox

Among relay events, **4x100m** has much higher clear-rates than **4x400m**:
- Men: 4x100m 26.3% vs. 4x400m 9.9%
- Women: 4x100m 37.2% vs. 4x400m 18.0%

Yet **4x400m** is the modal RQ1C event in most disciplines because more
athletes have larger margins relative to the (lower) 4x400m bar — even when
fewer actually clear it. Without relays, no such paradox exists.

### Change 6: Distance pairwise conclusions mostly persist

Core non-relay distance findings survive relay inclusion:
- Men 1500m vs. 5000m: 5000m still wins margin among dual athletes (225 vs.
  209 without; 225 vs. 209 with — unchanged in relay analysis).
- Women 1500m vs. 5000m: 5000m still wins (79 vs. 65).
- Steeplechase still beats 1500m among dual athletes (132–39 men; 75–6 women).

What changes: steeplechase and 800m lose RQ1C **recommendation share** to
relays (e.g., men steeple RQ1C assignments 209 → 165) even when steeple
head-to-heads vs. 1500m are unchanged.

### Change 7: Discipline athlete counts increase substantially

Relay legs add events and participants, especially outside sprints:

| Discipline | Men athletes (without → with) |
|------------|-------------------------------|
| Sprints    | 1,248 → 1,830 |
| Distance   | 1,933 → 2,518 |
| Hurdles    | 238 → 1,228 |
| Jumps      | 318 → 1,325 |
| Throws     | 191 → 1,285 |

Hurdles/jumps/throws counts rise sharply because discipline relay CSVs include
all relay leg athletes (822 men with 4x400m results), not only discipline
specialists. Interpret RQ1C modal relay recommendations in those disciplines
as **roster-wide relay opportunity**, not “hurdlers should quit hurdles.”


======================================================================
CROSS-RQ SYNTHESIS — PRACTICAL DIFFERENCES
======================================================================

### For RQ1A (descriptive specialization)
**Without relays:** Athletes specialize in individual events; findings align with
traditional event labels (miler, sprinter, jumper, etc.).

**With relays:** Specialization labels are systematically shifted toward 4x400m
(and secondarily 4x100m). The research question still asks “which event are
they best at?” but the answer set is fundamentally different — team scoring
blurs individual ability with relay assignment.

### For RQ1B (nationals distributions)
**Without relays:** Benchmark table covers 14 individual events per gender.

**With relays:** Same individual benchmarks **plus** two relay benchmarks that
are relatively accessible. Enables RQ1C relay comparisons but does not alter
any prior individual-event nationals characterization.

### For RQ1C (normative competitiveness)
**Without relays:** Recommendations stay within discipline individual events
(e.g., pursue 400m vs. 100m; pursue steeple vs. 1500m; field events with high
clear-rates stand out).

**With relays:** Normative advice often points to relays — especially 4x400m —
even for athletes whose primary discipline is jumps, throws, or hurdles. The
**actionable** relay target with the most nationals clearances is usually
**4x100m**, not the modal RQ1C event. Coaches must separate:
  (a) highest WA mark (RQ1A, often 4x400m),
  (b) best margin vs. threshold (RQ1C, often 4x400m),
  (c) highest probability path by population clears (often 4x100m).

### Hypothesis / paper framing impact (from Research_Questions.md)
- **H1C-1** (RQ1A ≠ RQ1C): Still supported with relays, but mismatch patterns
  change — distance women agreement rises; men's sprints agreement falls.
- **H1C-2** (gender distance asymmetry): **Unchanged** in 1500m vs. 5000m
  pairwise margins; relay inclusion does not overturn women's 5000m lean.
- **H1C-3** (steeple conditional advantage): Still supported in head-to-heads,
  but steeple loses share of top RQ1C assignments to relays.
- **RQ4** (relay robustness): This comparison **is** the RQ4 sensitivity
  analysis — relay inclusion materially changes RQ1A and RQ1C conclusions while
  leaving individual RQ1B intact.
